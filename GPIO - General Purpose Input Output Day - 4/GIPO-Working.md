Inside a microchip, a GPIO (General-Purpose Input/Output) pin connects to a pair of tiny electronic switches called MOSFET transistors. Setting a pin HIGH or LOW via software turns one of these microscopic transistors on, creating a physical pathway for electrons to flow either out from the chip's power rail or into ground.Inside the Transistor GateWhen you program a pin as an output and set it high (like 3.3V), the top transistor opens. This connects the external pin directly to the chip's internal voltage supply through a very small resistance. If you connect an external device like an LED and a resistor to ground, electrons flow out of the pin, through your circuit, and complete the loop.Sourcing Current: The pin pushes positive voltage/current out to the external load.Sinking Current: The bottom transistor turns on, pulling the pin down to 0V and letting electrons flow from an external source into the pin and down to the chip's ground.Input Mode: When set as an input, both transistors turn off. This creates a high-impedance state, meaning the pin acts like an open door with massive resistance. It draws almost zero current, only looking at the voltage level (like a tiny electrical pressure sensor) pushed against the gate by an outside source
